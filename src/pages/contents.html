Nine things that come back

Each chapter takes one cycle, says why it returns, and works its figures out in your browser. Read them in order, or by the length of the wait.

The chapters

Shortest to longest

The same nine, ranked by how long each takes to come round once. The bars are drawn on a logarithmic scale: each step to the right is ten times as long as the last, so a week and a month sit close together and a million years is only three steps beyond a thousand. On an ordinary scale every bar but Easter’s would be too short to see.

Each length is the one its chapter works out. The Hebrew cycle is 235 months of the molad, 6,939.69 days; Venus’s star is five of its synodic periods, a little short of eight years; Easter’s is the full Gregorian reckoning, after which the dates repeat in the same order. Only the week follows nothing in the sky.
